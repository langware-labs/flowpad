# Criterion: voice

> Ground rules (inline by design): never delete knowledge; integrate, don't
> append; max 5 findings/corrections per pass; converged is a valid verdict.

A skill is read by a capable model, not executed by a compiler. Phrasing that
transfers understanding outperforms phrasing that demands compliance.

1. **Write the move, not the prohibition.** "Run the tests before claiming
   done" beats "don't skip the tests": a prohibition leaves the right move
   unstated, and prohibitions don't compose — you'd need one per wrong
   variant, while one positive move excludes the whole space at once.
2. **Prohibitions are reserved for irreversible actions** — deleting, force
   pushes, sending to the outside world. There a hard stop is correct;
   everywhere else, name the move.
3. **Explain the why.** A rule with its reason survives novel situations; a
   bare MUST only survives the exact case its author imagined. Dense ALL-CAPS
   MUST/NEVER stacking is a finding in itself — it signals rules that were
   never understood, only feared.
4. **Imperative voice** for instructions. Descriptions of what "should
   happen" leave the actor ambiguous.
